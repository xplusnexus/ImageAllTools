Image cropping removes unwanted outer areas from a photo to improve composition, change the aspect ratio, or focus on a specific subject. Unlike resizing, which scales the whole image, cropping physically removes pixels to reframe it.

## How to crop images online

1. Upload a JPG, PNG, or WebP file.
2. Drag the crop handles for a custom selection, or lock a preset ratio (1:1, 16:9, 4:3, 4:5, 9:16, and more).
3. Preview the result and reposition by dragging inside the crop box.
4. Download — output keeps the original format for maximum compatibility.

## Popular use cases

- **Social media** — Instagram square/portrait, Facebook cover, X/Twitter headers, LinkedIn banners.
- **YouTube thumbnails** — crop to the required 16:9 (1280×720).
- **Passport & ID photos** — precise headshot cropping to government-required dimensions.
- **E-commerce** — uniform product photo dimensions across a catalog.
- **Profile pictures** — square or circular crops for WhatsApp, Zoom, Discord, Slack, GitHub.
- **Removing distractions** — cut out photobombers or busy backgrounds to tighten composition.

## Composition tips

- **Rule of thirds** — align the subject with grid intersections rather than dead center for more visual tension.
- **Leading lines** — crop so natural lines (roads, fences, edges) guide the eye toward the subject.
- **Fill the frame** — for portraits and product shots, cropping tight often reads as more intentional than including empty background.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Cropping too small from a low-resolution source (produces pixelation); mixing inconsistent aspect ratios across a single gallery; cropping right at a joint (knee, elbow, neck) in a portrait, which reads as an accidental "amputation."
